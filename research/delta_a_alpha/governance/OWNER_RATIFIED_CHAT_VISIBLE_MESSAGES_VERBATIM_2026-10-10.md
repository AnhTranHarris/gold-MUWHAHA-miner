## How we prevent another research restart

I will apply four explicit rules.

1. **January and February Excellence remain intact.** Their original mechanisms, settings, source code, and discoveries are preserved. They are not averaged together or replaced with generalized parameters.

2. **Trading behavior must be conditioned on observable market information.** Settings and mechanisms can differ across market conditions when supported by research. Those conditions must be identified causally—not by knowing which historical month produced the highest profit.

3. **Adaptation is evaluated as a complete system.** New mechanisms must be tested through the shared portfolio, where capital constraints, existing positions, execution competition, gross loss, and drawdown all interact. We do not optimize individual layers at the expense of the system.

4. **Every successful discovery advances the existing implementation.** We preserve working code and configurations, add condition-aware improvements, and validate their interactions. We do not restart from scratch simply because the next market behaves differently.

One important technical safeguard: we should distinguish **adaptation based on information available during trading** from hindsight optimization based on future outcomes. The first is the capability we want; the second produces misleading backtests.

## The central research objective

*How do we make February or March behave like January?*

**What market conditions made January Excellence possible? What different conditions made February Excellence possible? That question allows the system to improve progressively without sacrificing what earlier research established.

It also changes how we judge future discoveries. A new mechanism does not need to resemble January or February. It needs to contribute useful, verifiable intelligence or improve the unified portfolio's actual performance.

**Our principle is adaptive trading intelligence within disciplined execution boundaries—not rigid trading behavior imposed across changing markets.**
