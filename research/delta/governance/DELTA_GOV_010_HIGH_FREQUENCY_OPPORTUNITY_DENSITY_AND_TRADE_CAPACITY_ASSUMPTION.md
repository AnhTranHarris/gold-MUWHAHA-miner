# DELTA GOV-010 — High-Frequency Opportunity Density and Incremental Trade-Capacity Assumption

**Effective:** 2026-10-01  
**Branch:** `delta`  
**Status:** MANDATORY / HUMAN-REVISABLE  
**Evidence loading:** NOT AUTHORIZED

## 1. Owner-defined architecture assumption

DELTA assumes the eventual XAUUSD system is a **high-trading-frequency composite** whose edge depends materially on finding and exploiting a large number of valid daily trade opportunities.

This is an owner-defined DELTA design assumption. It does not import code, metrics, logic, or conclusions from any prior internal research lineage.

The objective is therefore not only:

`make each trade better`

but also:

`find more distinct causal opportunities that the composite can exploit without violating quality, drawdown, loss, or locked-metric constraints`.

## 2. Opportunity density is a first-class system objective

DELTA treats opportunity density and trade capacity as first-class research objectives.

Every candidate must report, where measurable:
- raw opportunity count;
- unique opportunity count;
- net-new opportunities versus its declared parent/composite;
- overlap with existing specialists;
- cannibalized opportunities;
- routed opportunities;
- executed trade count;
- winning trade count;
- opportunities per trading day;
- trades per trading day;
- opportunity-to-trade conversion rate.

Primary human metrics remain:
- winning trade count;
- net profit;
- gross loss;
- maximum drawdown.

Opportunity density does not replace those metrics. It explains whether the system is expanding its usable edge surface.

## 3. Every accepted specialist must contribute opportunity capacity

An accepted specialist should add positive incremental opportunity capacity to the DELTA composite.

The default test is:

`net_new_unique_opportunities = composite_with_candidate_unique_opportunities - parent_composite_unique_opportunities`

A specialist intended for promotion should normally satisfy:

`net_new_unique_opportunities > 0`

and should identify where those opportunities come from:
- a previously uncovered session;
- a previously uncovered regime;
- a news/event state;
- a different timeframe interaction;
- a different entry/hold/exit mechanism;
- a previously rejected opportunity made viable by better execution/risk handling.

A candidate that produces only duplicate proposals already captured by the existing composite has not expanded the system's opportunity surface.

## 4. Unique opportunity, duplicate opportunity, and cannibalization

DELTA must distinguish:

### Unique opportunity
A causal executable opportunity that the parent composite would not otherwise have proposed/taken under the same market chronology and ownership rules.

### Duplicate opportunity
A proposal materially representing the same executable market opportunity already generated by another specialist.

### Cannibalized opportunity
A previously available opportunity that disappears, is blocked, or becomes untradeable because the new candidate changes routing, ownership, timing, risk, or state.

Every composition test must measure both gains and losses:

`net_opportunity_gain = unique_added - unique_cannibalized`

Raw proposal count alone is insufficient.

## 5. High-frequency does not mean indiscriminate trading

DELTA's high-frequency assumption does not authorize:
- duplicate spam entries;
- repeated entries without distinct causal opportunity;
- ignoring spread/slippage;
- lowering execution quality simply to inflate count;
- violating risk or metric locks;
- synthetic opportunity creation from future information.

The target is **high valid opportunity density**, not artificial trade-count inflation.

## 6. Candidate value in a multi-specialist system

Under GOV-008, specialists may be narrow.

A candidate can add value by expanding opportunity coverage in:
- Australia;
- Asia;
- Russia;
- India;
- Middle East;
- Europe;
- UK;
- New York;
- overlapping sessions;
- medium/high-impact news periods;
- volatility regimes;
- trend/range/reversal states;
- liquidity/path states.

A narrow specialist need not dominate all primary metrics if it contributes distinct opportunity capacity and integrates positively into the composite.

## 7. Protective mechanisms and opportunity capacity

A mechanism whose direct purpose is protection rather than entry generation can still satisfy the opportunity-capacity principle if it causally enables more valid trades elsewhere.

Examples:
- spread/liquidity protection that prevents catastrophic fills while allowing trading to resume sooner;
- drawdown-control logic that preserves capacity for later opportunities;
- event routing that converts a blanket blackout into selective tradable windows;
- conflict resolution that permits two complementary specialists to operate without destructive overlap.

Such a mechanism must demonstrate **enabled or recovered opportunity capacity** at the composite level.

A mechanism that only suppresses trades without measurable compensating system benefit should not be treated as an opportunity-expanding specialist.

## 8. Daily opportunity surface

Because the system is intended to trade frequently, scorecards should report opportunity/trade density by day where the data permits.

At minimum:
- active trading days;
- median opportunities/day;
- mean opportunities/day;
- median trades/day;
- mean trades/day;
- zero-opportunity days;
- zero-trade days;
- session contribution;
- event/news contribution.

The purpose is to detect candidates that look strong only because they trade rarely.

## 9. Interaction with GOV-005

GOV-005's 80% hard improvement gate, 85% preferred target, 17% warning, and 25% hard deterioration floor remain active.

GOV-010 adds an opportunity-capacity expectation.

A candidate should not be preferred merely because it improves a headline primary metric while materially shrinking the usable opportunity surface.

Until the owner defines a numeric minimum opportunity-growth threshold, DELTA must report opportunity expansion explicitly and flag candidates with non-positive net-new opportunity contribution as:

`OPPORTUNITY_EXPANSION_NOT_DEMONSTRATED`

This flag requires human review before promotion.

## 10. Interaction with GOV-006 locks

Opportunity expansion must preserve every inherited composite metric lock.

Adding more opportunities is not a valid improvement if the resulting composite violates the 5% locked-metric preservation rule.

Thus DELTA searches for:

`more valid opportunities + preserved locked performance`

rather than:

`more trades at any cost`.

## 11. Interaction with GOV-007 creative refinement

GOV-007 may use opportunity density as a creative-search objective.

When a candidate's metric progress or opportunity expansion stalls, DELTA may:
- create additional specialists;
- split one specialist into narrower opportunity hunters;
- combine complementary entry mechanisms;
- alter session ownership;
- add event/news specialists;
- redesign routing to reduce duplicate/cannibalized proposals;
- search different nested-timeframe structures;
- redesign hold/exit logic so more opportunities can be exploited without degrading loss/drawdown.

Repeated bounded Python search is allowed once data loading is authorized.

## 12. Interaction with GOV-009 news/event handling

Medium/high-impact news is explicitly part of the opportunity surface.

News/event specialists should be evaluated not only on profitability/risk but also on whether they convert otherwise unusable or blacked-out event windows into valid incremental opportunities.

## 13. Composite-level accounting

The composite scorecard must reconcile:

`sum(raw specialist opportunities)`

against:

`unique composite opportunities`

and:

`executed composite trades`.

This prevents double counting.

The report must explain:
- duplicate proposals;
- router suppression;
- ownership conflicts;
- simultaneous opportunities;
- lost/cannibalized opportunities;
- opportunities rejected for execution/risk reasons.

## 14. Human-review authority

This rule is human-revisable.

The owner may later define:
- minimum net-new opportunities per candidate;
- minimum opportunities/day;
- minimum trades/day;
- minimum opportunity retention;
- acceptable duplication rate;
- acceptable cannibalization;
- session-specific opportunity floors;
- event-specific opportunity floors.

Until numeric floors are defined, positive net-new opportunity contribution remains the intended direction and must be explicitly measured.
